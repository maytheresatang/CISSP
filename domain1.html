Domain 1 is the largest domain at 16% of the exam. It tests your ability to think like a security leader — not a technician. The domain anchors every other CISSP domain: governance, risk, and ethics form the lens through which all security decisions are made.

The CIA Triad

🔒 Confidentiality

Prevent unauthorised disclosure. Controls: encryption, access control, classification, NDA.

Attack: snooping, eavesdropping, shoulder surfing, data theft

✅ Integrity

Prevent unauthorised modification. Controls: hashing (MD5, SHA), digital signatures, checksums, version control.

Attack: data tampering, man-in-the-middle, malware

📶 Availability

Ensure authorised users can access resources when needed. Controls: redundancy, failover, RAID, load balancing, DDoS mitigation, BCP/DRP.

Attack: DoS/DDoS, ransomware, natural disaster, hardware failure

Extended Security Concepts
ConceptDefinition
Non-repudiationProof that a party performed an action and cannot deny it. Works both ways: proof of origin (sender cannot deny sending — digital signatures) and proof of delivery (recipient cannot deny receiving — signed delivery receipt, certified mail).
AuthenticityVerifying identity of a user or system (who you say you are).
PrivacyRight of individuals to control personal information.
AccountabilityTracking actions back to a specific identity (audit logs).
⚠️ Exam Trap: Availability vs Confidentiality Priority In most civilian/commercial scenarios, Confidentiality > Integrity > Availability. In safety-critical systems (hospitals, power grids, OT/ICS) Availability often trumps Confidentiality. Read the scenario carefully.
🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
Haruto sends legal documents by certified mail and requests a recipient signature on delivery, so he will have paperwork confirming the documents were delivered. This is an example of?
✅ Nonrepudiation
The signed receipt is proof of delivery — the recipient cannot later deny receiving the documents.
  • Accountability = tracing actions to an identity (audit logs). Nonrepudiation supports it, but the question is about undeniable proof.
  • Authentication = verifying identity before granting access.
  • "Confidentiality upon delivery" — certified mail does nothing to hide the contents.
Trap: don't think nonrepudiation = digital signatures only. Any mechanism producing undeniable proof (signatures, signed receipts, notarised documents) counts.
Risk Formula Essentials
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
SLE (Single Loss Expectancy) = Asset Value × Exposure Factor (EF) ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence) = how often per year ALE (Annual Loss Expectancy) = SLE × ARO ALE after control = ALE before – ALE after – Cost of control
📌 Worked Example Asset value: $200,000. Fire destroys 40% → EF = 0.4 → SLE = $80,000. Fire occurs once in 5 years → ARO = 0.2. ALE = $80,000 × 0.2 = $16,000/year. A sprinkler system costing $10,000/year that reduces ALE to $2,000 saves $4,000 net — worth implementing.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Residual vs Total Risk Total Risk = Threats × Vulnerabilities × Asset Value (no controls). Residual Risk = what remains AFTER controls. The exam often asks about residual risk — always "after controls are applied."

🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
When discussing risk analysis, which of the following BEST describes weakness?
✅ Vulnerability
  • "Likelihood and impact of a threat occurring in the organisation's operating context" is the definition of risk, not weakness.
  • CVE is a naming system for specific known vulnerabilities — too narrow.
  • Risk combines threat + vulnerability + impact; the weakness itself is the vulnerability.
Memory hook: Threat = what could happen · Vulnerability = the weak spot · Risk = how likely × how bad.
ISC2 Code of Ethics — Canon Order (CRITICAL)
🚨 Most Missed Question — Memorise This Order Canons are listed in PRIORITY order. If two canons conflict, the higher-numbered one yields to the lower.
Mnemonic
P · C · P · S
Protect Society → Act Honourably (Client) → Provide Competent Service (Profession) → Advance the Profession (Self)
#CanonWhat it means
1Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructurePublic safety FIRST — above all clients and employers
2Act honourably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legallyIntegrity in all professional dealings
3Provide diligent and competent service to principalsCompetence and diligence for clients/employers
4Advance and protect the professionSelf and profession last
⚠️ Exam Trap: Ethics in Action If you discover your employer is committing fraud, you do not handle it internally first. Canon 1 (public good) supersedes Canon 3 (client service). You must escalate externally if public harm is involved. Do NOT destroy evidence — preserve it.

🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
Of the following, which is NOT a type of law that exists within the Common legal category of law? (Criminal · Tort · Congressional · Administrative)
✅ Congressional law
The common-law system (US, UK, Canada, Australia) has three main categories: criminal, civil (which includes tort and contract law) and administrative (regulatory) law. "Congressional law" is not a category — Congress is the body that passes statutes, and those statutes land in one of the three categories.
  • Other legal systems to recognise: civil (code) law — continental Europe; religious law; customary law; mixed systems.

BCP vs DRP — Key Distinction

📋 BCP (Business Continuity Plan)

  • Keeps business operating during disruption
  • Business process focus
  • Strategic / organisational
  • Longer time horizon
  • Includes DRP as a sub-component

🔧 DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan)

  • Restores IT systems after disruption
  • Technical / IT focus
  • Tactical / operational
  • Shorter time horizon
  • Sub-plan within BCP
Critical BCP/DRP Terminology
TermDefinitionExam Tip
RTO (Recovery Time Objective)Max acceptable downtime before service must be restoredRTO drives your recovery strategy choice
RPO (Recovery Point Objective)Max acceptable data loss measured in time (how old can your backup be?)RPO drives backup frequency
MTD / MAD (Maximum Tolerable / Allowable Downtime)Absolute maximum time before irreparable harmMTD > RTO always. If RTO exceeds MTD = failure.
MTTR (Mean Time To Repair)Average time to fix a failed componentLower is better
MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)Average time between failures of a systemHigher is better
WRT (Work Recovery Time)Time to restore business functions after systems are backRTO + WRT ≤ MTD

🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
Jacob's team is building a disaster recovery plan and is considering a reciprocal agreement for a failover site. Which BEST describes a reciprocal agreement?
✅ An agreement between two organisations that allows each to use the other's data centre during a disaster
Also called a Mutual Assistance Agreement (MAA): two organisations pledge to host each other's computing workload if one suffers a disaster. Cheap, but rarely relied on because:
  • Hard to enforce — it's a promise, not a contract with SLAs.
  • Both organisations may be hit by the same regional disaster.
  • The host may not have spare capacity; hosting another company's data raises confidentiality concerns.
Distractors: a line of credit is a financial arrangement; office space alone misses the point (it's about processing capability); "no competitive pressure" is invented.
Contingency Plan Types (NIST SP 800-34)
PlanFocus
BCP (Business Continuity Plan)Sustains business processes during and after a disruption — scope goes beyond just the mission essential functions
COOP (Continuity of Operations Plan)Restores an organisation's Mission Essential Functions (MEFs) at an alternate site and sustains them for up to 30 days. US federal origin (FEMA).
DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan)Restores IT systems/facilities at an alternate site after a major disruption
ISCP (Information System Contingency Plan)Recovery of a single information system
OEP (Occupant Emergency Plan)Protects people in a facility — evacuation, shelter-in-place
Crisis Communications PlanInternal and external messaging during an incident
🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
What is the difference between a COOP and a BCP?
✅ A BCP focuses on processes and tasks beyond mission essential functions (MEFs), while a COOP is primarily focused on MEFs
COOP = the essential core only (MEFs, alternate site, up to 30 days). BCP = the broader business, including processes that aren't mission-essential. The "security vs privacy" options are invented — neither plan is defined by security or privacy.
Memory Hook
COOP = Core Only · BCP = Broader
🎯 Practice Q Review · Missed / Unsure
The HR database must be restored from backups; IT says this will take about 24 hours. HR management has already told staff that operations will cease tomorrow and they need not report to work — even though IT has not started the restore. What is this result MOST LIKELY due to?
✅ Business continuity planning
Deciding how the business operates (or pauses) during an outage — including what staff should do — is a pre-planned BCP decision built on BIA data (MTD, RTO). The DRP covers the technical restore that IT is about to perform. The fact that management already knew the business response before IT began restoring points to an existing continuity plan, not IT staff experience or an ad hoc risk/reward call.
  • Scenario signal: people / business processes → BCP. Systems / data restoration → DRP.

BCP Process Phases
  1. Project Initiation — Scope, policy, senior management support, BCP committee
  2. BIA (Business Impact Analysis) — Identify critical processes, dependencies, MTD, RTO, RPO, financial impact
  3. Recovery Strategy Selection — Choose controls and alternate sites based on BIA output
  4. Plan Development — Document procedures, assign roles, establish communication trees
  5. Testing & Maintenance — Test types below; review annually or after major change
BCP Test Types (Low → High Disruption)
Test TypeDescriptionDisruption
Checklist / Document ReviewReview plan documents for completenessNone
Tabletop ExerciseTalk through scenario with key stakeholdersNone
Walkthrough / Structured Walk-throughTeam walks through plan step by stepVery low
SimulationRehearsal without activating recovery systemsLow
Parallel TestRecovery systems activated alongside production (both run)Medium
Full Interruption TestProduction shut down; recovery site takes overHighest — rarely done
✅ Core Concept The BIA output drives everything: MTD → site selection, RPO → backup frequency, RTO → recovery strategy. The CISSP frames BCP/DR exactly this way.
